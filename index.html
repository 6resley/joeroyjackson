The man behind the music.

“The quintessential American blues man.”

Don Wilcock, New York Blues Society

Joe Roy picked up the guitar at six, growing up in a musical family. His mother, Mary Jackson, played lap steel and taught him the fundamentals of slide. His father, Keith Jackson Sr., played lead guitar with local country musicians and gave Joe Roy his first guitar.

Short bio 

   
Joe Roy Jackson is a blues guitarist from Schenectady, New York. He picked up the guitar at six, learning slide from his mother, lap steel player Mary Jackson, and lead from his father, Keith Jackson Sr. A two-time winner of Upstate New York’s “King of the Blues” (2006, 2008), he played the 2010 Jamaica Jazz & Blues Festival alongside Joss Stone and Maxi Priest and was inducted into the New York State Blues Hall of Fame in 2013. He released the albums Back to the Blues (1992), Hot Stuff (1995) and So You Think It’s Easy Being Me (2012) and the five-song EP Brothers in 2019, and in 2026 he won Favorite Blues Artist at the Listen Up Awards.
